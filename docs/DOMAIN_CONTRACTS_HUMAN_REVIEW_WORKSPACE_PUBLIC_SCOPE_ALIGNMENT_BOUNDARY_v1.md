## 1. Purpose and source boundary

This boundary freezes the smallest canonical product description included in
the public source release.

Commercial strategy, launch planning, investor material, financing, pricing,
market-size estimates, buyer-demand claims, named-organization claims, and
public-sector discovery material are excluded from this public repository.
Their exclusion creates no negative or positive conclusion about those topics.

Tracked repository docs, contracts, schemas, tests, and runtime evidence retain
their existing source-of-truth roles. Untracked advisory material cannot alter
this public boundary.

## 2. Canonical product framing

The canonical product direction is layered:

1. `GOVERNANCE_NO_OVERCLAIM_KERNEL`: fail-closed boundaries, declared source
   limits, review-state separation, stop behavior, traceability, and human
   control.
2. `HUMAN_REVIEW_WORKSPACE`: a workspace target for organizing authorized,
   supplied sensitive digital material into source-linked review structures and
   controlled handoff support.

The bounded product description is:

> A source-bound human review workspace that helps professional reviewers turn
> authorized, supplied messages and documents into a correctable review
> chronology, asserted-claim matrix, declared packet/review gaps, human review
> questions, and a controlled handoff brief without creating legal,
> evidentiary, ownership, credibility, source-truth, or case-truth conclusions.

`HUMAN_REVIEW_WORKSPACE` describes a product-contract target. It does not prove
that runtime, schemas, routes, storage, security controls, exports, or user
interfaces currently implement that target.

## 3. Canonical bounded workflow target

The intended workflow target is partitioned as follows:

1. Declare an authorized and bounded supplied packet. The workspace does not
   acquire a device, obtain account access, or perform forensic extraction.
2. Create a source register using stable opaque references to supplied items.
3. Propose a neutral, cross-source review chronology that a human reviewer can
   correct, reject, or annotate.
4. Keep asserted claims separate from what appears in the supplied material.
5. Surface declared packet/review gaps, conflicts, missing context, and possible
   human review questions.
6. Require human correction and approval before any controlled handoff or
   export candidate.
7. Preserve attributable boundary stops, corrections, approval decisions, and
   export decisions when separately implemented and authorized.

The source-link target means an opaque reference within the declared supplied
packet. It does not authorize raw replay, private paths, filenames, URLs,
tokens, external source locators, authenticity claims, or chain-of-custody
claims.

## 4. Review-state distinction

The workspace target must preserve these four conceptual review states:

- `ASSERTED`: an asserted event or claim, without model endorsement.
- `APPEARS_IN_SUPPLIED_MATERIAL`: a bounded observation that specified content
  appears in the declared supplied packet, without source-truth or authenticity
  conclusion.
- `NOT_ESTABLISHED`: the requested proposition is not established by the model
  under the current boundary.
- `HUMAN_REVIEW_REQUIRED`: a human or professional reviewer must decide the
  relevant question.

These are review-state distinctions, not a runtime enum or schema. They must
not be converted into probability, credibility, reliability, merit,
sufficiency, guilt, ownership, or court-usefulness scores.

## 5. Controlled output families

The bounded workspace target may support these review-only output families when
separately implemented and authorized:

- `SOURCE_REGISTER`
- `REVIEW_CHRONOLOGY`
- `ASSERTED_CLAIM_MATRIX`
- `DECLARED_PACKET_REVIEW_GAPS`
- `HUMAN_REVIEW_QUESTIONS`
- `NO_CONCLUSION_NOTICE`
- `CONTROLLED_HANDOFF_BRIEF`

Every output remains a proposal for human review. No output selects final
evidence, certifies evidence, prepares a filing-ready legal conclusion, or
bypasses human/professional review.

## 6. Language and no-conclusion rule

Preferred language includes:

- asserted claim
- appears in supplied material
- declared packet/review gap
- possible human review question
- not established by the model
- no model conclusion under the current boundary
- may require human/professional review
- human-review readiness only
- not evidentiary sufficiency
- not an ownership determination

The workspace must stop or reframe requests to:

- rank evidence, material, or source signals as strong, weak, credible,
  reliable, likely, sufficient, persuasive, meritorious, or useful in court
- infer source truth, identity truth, authorship truth, completeness,
  authenticity, intent, guilt, ownership, or chain-of-custody truth
- predict legal merit, legal outcome, court outcome, risk, or protection outcome
- select or certify final evidence
- create a court-ready, police-ready, filing-ready, or decision-ready conclusion
- convert human-review readiness into evidentiary sufficiency, approval,
  closure, readiness, certification, or sign-off

Older domain-specific review-signal wording remains historical context within
its own tracked boundary. It must not be used to introduce evidence-strength or
source-strength ranking into the Human Review Workspace product boundary.

## 7. Public release exclusions

This public repository does not include or establish:

- commercial strategy, launch plans, pricing, financing, investor asks, market
  sizing, buyer targeting, sales hypotheses, or organizational narratives
- private recipient communications, review packets, dossiers, delivery plans,
  meeting material, or non-disclosure material
- product-market validation, procurement evidence, endorsement, deployment
  suitability, or operational-readiness claims

These exclusions are publication boundaries only. They do not select a product
candidate, create market truth, authorize external use, or approve release.

## 8. High-impact domain posture

The Human Review Workspace does not:

- acquire, unlock, preserve, image, or forensically extract devices or accounts
- replace forensic, evidence-management, case-management, investigative,
  prosecutorial, defence, judicial, or professional-review systems or roles
- establish authenticity, completeness, authorship, chain of custody,
  credibility, intent, guilt, legal threshold, or evidentiary sufficiency
- select final evidence or generate a court-ready or police-ready conclusion

Police, court, prosecution, child-welfare, public-sector, and other high-impact
uses are outside this public release. Any future evaluation requires a separate
explicit Owner decision and the applicable professional, legal, privacy,
security, procurement, human-factors, and organizational review.

## 9. Security, privacy, and real-data prerequisites

Synthetic or sanitized material remains the default evaluation boundary. This
document does not authorize real private material, real evidence, source
packages, screenshots, device data, or third-party model routing.

Before any separately authorized real-data evaluation, tracked evidence would
be required for at least:

- tenant, matter, and case isolation
- role-based and least-privilege access control
- attributable audit and correction history
- encryption and key-management posture
- retention, deletion, and erasure controls
- redaction and no-raw output controls
- incident handling and accountable ownership
- export approval and recipient boundary
- third-party processor and routing status
- documented model limits and fail-closed stop behavior

These are prerequisites and unresolved gates, not implemented-control claims.
EU AI Act and GDPR references are regulatory design context only. They do not
create compliance, conformity, lawful-basis, data-protection, or security
certification.
